# Skill: discovery-context (ex build-context, ex post-briefing, POST)

## 1. Role

After the briefing meeting, you expand the context with what actually came out of it and build the project/client's internal context. From that context, a client-facing pre-proposal is derived. The output is larger than briefing-context; it's not the prep corrected, it's the prep expanded. End goal: give the brief real leverage in closing the deal.

## 2. Where in the process

Stage **1-PreSale** (see [`docs/skills-roadmap.md`](../../docs/skills-roadmap.md)), right after the briefing meeting, starting from the briefing transcript plus any client documentation. See the canonical map: [`briefings/_ciclo-preventa.md`](../../briefings/_ciclo-preventa.md).

Careful with "discovery": three different things share the word (see `_ciclo-preventa.md` § Discovery != briefing != discovery-context). When the pre-proposal (section 6, block B) sells a discovery, it refers to the post-sale/pre-development stage (several paid sessions), not the briefing meeting and not this skill.

**Timing rule:** the pre-proposal should go out within 1-2 days of the meeting; don't let it go cold. Reserve 15 minutes right after the meeting to process the output and create the task, instead of chaining meetings and writing up notes at night.

## 3. Required input

- The Preparation Document (output of `/briefing-context`).
- Answers obtained in the meeting.
- Transcript (Granola / Meet).
- Documents sent by the client.
- Info created by us: what came out of internal meetings or our own judgment (not just the client's input).
- Any other info that came up.

## 4. What it does, step by step

**The three layers, so you don't mix them up:**
1. **Project context (this skill's main output): internal.** Never delivered to the client. The working input: expanded problem, context, users, stakeholders, roles, feasibility, and how we'd work it (methodological context).
2. **Pre-proposal / draft plan: client-facing (light).** Derived from the project context: "here's what we saw, here's the problem, here's the solution we propose, here's a demo/prototype of what we're imagining, are you interested?" No locked-in team/cost/timeline.
3. **Real proposal: only once there's a "yes".** Team, timeline, cost, discovery, who's in charge. This skill does not build it; it leaves the project context ready and the pre-proposal pre-assembled.

**Working principles:**
- Expand, don't rewrite from scratch. Start from the Preparation Document and add what's new to it.
- Be honest about feasibility. If we don't have the experience, API, or access, say so; it can still be proposed.
- Be precise and actionable. No placeholders, no ambiguity. What's left open is named as open, not filled in.
- Keep internal vs. client-facing always separated. The context is internal; the pre-proposal is what's shown.

**Step 1: resolve the information goals**, now with data from the meeting:
1. **Real problem (expanded)**: what we understood before vs. what we understand now; name what changed.
2. **New context**: systems, constraints, numbers, priorities, who decides.
3. **Users / roles / permissions**: confirmed or corrected.
4. **Feasibility**: can we help? with what (experience, API, access)? what's missing?
5. **Stakeholders**: who each one is and what role they play.
6. **How we'd work it**: the methodological context.

**Step 2: revalidate the 3 mandatory analysis axes** with what came out of the meeting, and go deep on whichever is the crux:
- **Regulatory**: what was confirmed about the bodies/regulations that reach the solution.
- **Legal framework**: what can/can't be done (data, delegation, contracts).
- **Tech stack**: real stack, possible integrations, what's reusable vs. what needs rebuilding.

**Step 3: build the output**, in three blocks (see section 6 for full contents):
- **Block A, project context (internal)**: problem statement, expanded context, users/roles/permissions, stakeholders, methodological context.
- **Block B, pre-proposal (client-facing, light)**: scope, discovery sell, prototype if feasible, how we'd work it, feasibility verdict.
- **Block C, operational output**: identify (don't draft) the meeting notes and next steps; the notes themselves are generated by calling `/minutero`.

For the methodological context specifically, use the reusable template [`briefings/_contexto-metodologico.md`](../../briefings/_contexto-metodologico.md): standard Quarks baseline plus per-client adjustment (full management vs. with dependencies); each dependency gets an owner and a risk-if-unresolved.

**Step 4: update project-context and client-context.** Append a Timeline entry to `briefings/<client>-project-context.md` (see [`briefings/_project-context-template.md`](../../briefings/_project-context-template.md)) and update `knowledge/org/<client-slug>.md` (client-context) with anything durably learned this round (a confirmed stakeholder, a system constraint, a regulatory axis). Both should already exist from `/briefing-context`; if they don't, create them now rather than skipping the step.

## 5. What it does NOT do

- Does not build the real proposal (team/timeline/cost lock-in); that only happens post-"yes".
- Does not draft the meeting notes itself; it identifies them as an output and calls `/minutero`.
- Does not mix internal and client-facing content; the context is internal, the pre-proposal is what's shown.
- Out of scope (v2): feeding the proposal with the team's prior experience by industry ("for this, talk to Irra/Nico/Juani"), depends on a skills map surveyed separately.
- Out of scope (v2): a reusable experience doc by domain (e.g. everything learned on a CRM project) for future clients of the same type.
- Out of scope (v2): day-to-day operational communication style per person (preferred channel, what causes friction, how to escalate, e.g. "Seba via WhatsApp not Discord"); deferred to a future `communication-context` skill.

## 6. What comes out

**A. Project context (internal)**
- **Problem statement (expanded)**: what we understood before vs. now, with what changed explicitly named.
- **Expanded context**: everything new: systems, constraints, numbers, priorities, decision-makers.
- **Users / roles / permissions**: refined with what was confirmed in the meeting.
- **Stakeholders, who each one is**: a mini profile per person, one line each: role in the project and what matters to them. Motivations and style are interpretations; label them and anchor them to the transcript/source they came from.
- **Methodological context, how we'd work it**: roles and responsibilities on both sides, environment management, communication model, reviews/documentation, SLA, and what absolutely needs defining.

**B. Pre-proposal (client-facing, light)**
- **Scope**: what's in, what's out.
- **Discovery**: 1 to 4 weeks depending on complexity, sold as "a team for this long/this cost, and first a discovery to make the best use of it"; discovery isn't sold standalone.
- **Prototype if feasible**: could be a website, an explanation, or a flow, not always a screen.
- **How we'd work it**: an excerpt from the methodological context, not just what we do but how.
- **Can we help or not?**: an honest feasibility verdict; if API/experience/access is missing, it goes here.

**C. Operational output**
- **Open items**: what was left unresolved.
- **Actions**: send the notes to the client right away, within 1-2 days max; create the tasks for the next deliverable.

Also: an updated `project-context` Timeline entry, and a refreshed `client-context`.

## 7. How it comes out

This skill's deliverable is always a formatted Google Doc, never a standalone `.md`. Two artifacts, in this order:

1. **Markdown backup (repo)**: the project context (blocks A/B/C) as markdown. Audit anchor, never deleted.
2. **Formatted Google Doc (deliverable)**: created from that same markdown via the Google Drive connector's `create_file`, `contentMimeType: text/markdown`, `disableConversionToGoogleType` left off so Drive converts it into a Google Doc with headings, bold, and tables.

This skill's Google Doc is internal (project context), not the one sent to the client; the client-facing proposal comes out of the `/propuestador` skill. The raw markdown is not the final deliverable; the deliverable is the Google Doc, and the `.md` stays in the repo purely for traceability.

## 8. Where it goes

- Markdown backup: `briefings/YYYY-MM-<client>-discovery-context.md`, plus the `source/` of the meeting when applicable.
- Google Doc: title `Project Context, <Client>, <YYYY-MM-DD>`; if the PM specified a Drive folder, pass its `parentId`, otherwise it goes to the root.
- `project-context`: `briefings/<client>-project-context.md` (append the Timeline entry).
- `client-context`: `knowledge/org/<client-slug>.md` (update, or create if `/briefing-context` never ran for this client).
- Close by returning the Google Doc link plus the path to the backup `.md`.

## 9. Example

**Input:** AL2/ACA Preparation Document + Granola transcript of the meeting + doc of the current onboarding process they sent over.

**How you interpret it:** in the prep, we assumed "unify wallet and brokerage onboarding." In the meeting it's confirmed that the crux is the regulatory axis: AL2's KYC (BCRA) can't be reused as-is to open ACA Valores' brokerage account (CNV/UIF, non-delegable AML). The real goal shifts to a single onboarding that branches, not a single sign-up.

**Output (excerpt):**
- *Expanded problem statement:* it's not "one sign-up for both"; it's a shared entry point that reuses shareable data and triggers two different KYC flows depending on the regulator.
- *Pre-proposal:* 3-week discovery; flow prototype (not a screen) mapping which data is requested only once and where it branches by regulation; integrates with each product's core.
- *Feasibility:* medium-high; we have fintech experience (AL2 is already a client); pending: identity API and what legal/UIF allows.
- *Stakeholder, Genaro (CEO):* business/embedded-finance decision-maker; cares about the business case and conversion.
- *Stakeholder, Nahuel (CTO, ex-Wenance):* technical role; cares about KYC/AML and integration.

## 10. Skill acceptance criteria

- The three layers stay separated: context (internal), pre-proposal (client-facing), real proposal (post-yes, later).
- The problem statement is expanded, not rebuilt from scratch; what changed vs. the prep is named.
- The 3 axes (regulatory/legal/stack) revalidated with data from the meeting.
- Methodological context present: roles/responsibilities on both sides, environments, communication, reviews, SLA.
- Pre-proposal includes scope, discovery (1-4 weeks), prototype-if-feasible, "how we work it," and an honest feasibility verdict.
- Meeting notes identified as an output that calls `/minutero`, not drafted here.
- Stakeholders listed with role and what matters to them; operational communication style deferred to the future `communication-context`.
- Motivations/styles labeled as interpretation and anchored to source.
- Deliverable is a formatted Google Doc (internal) via the Drive connector; markdown backup in the repo; link is returned.
- `project-context` and `client-context` are both updated (or created, if this is somehow the first touchpoint).
- No placeholders or inflated scope; output is 100% Latin American Spanish.
